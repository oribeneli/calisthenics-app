# The Program

This is the prose version of what `src/data/program.json` encodes. For the evidence behind every decision here, see [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) and the four research briefs it links — this page states the program, not the citations.

## Weekly template

Three identical full-body sessions per week, every other day, picked as three fixed day + time + place slots. Each session:

1. **Warm-up** (~5 min, fixed — see below)
2. **Five patterns, in order:** push → pull → squat → hinge → core, 1–3 sets each at the current level
3. **Cool-down** (optional, ~2 min)

Target session length: 20–25 minutes. Rest days get a 5-minute mobility routine instead of the session (see below), plus the six daily habit checkboxes.

A "short version (5 min)" — one set of each pattern — is always available and always counts as a completed session.

## Week-0 assessment protocol

Week 0 is an onboarding week, not a training week. After the screening questions (below) are cleared:

1. Start at **level 1** of every ladder.
2. Do **one clean set** at the target reps/hold.
3. If it felt easy, **step up one level** and repeat — but never above the ladder's assessment cap:
   - Push: capped at **P3**
   - Pull: capped at **L4**
   - Squat: capped at **S4**
   - Hinge: capped at **H3**
   - Core: capped at **C4**
4. Wherever the set stops feeling easy (or the cap is hit first), that is the starting level for week 1.

The push caps are firm because P1–P4 are scapular-control prerequisites, not optional warm-up filler — the assessment never places the user above them regardless of how easy the reps felt.

## Volume ramp and RPE ceiling

Sets per exercise increase as the habit is established; the intensity ceiling rises separately as tissue tolerance builds.

| From week | Sets per exercise |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 4 | 3 |

| From week | RPE ceiling |
|---|---|
| 0 | 6 |
| 3 | 7 |
| 9 | 8 |

The stop signal is never a self-estimated RIR — novices misjudge it by ~4–5 reps. The observable signal is "the last rep looks different from the first." Nothing is ever trained to failure in the first 8–12 weeks.

Rest between sets: 60 s for the scapular-control and isometric-hold levels, 90 s once the working sets get harder. Tempo default throughout: 2 s down, 1 s pause, 2 s up.

## The five ladders

Each ladder is ordered easiest to hardest. "Target" is the full-volume (week 4+) working set; week 0–3 uses fewer sets per the volume ramp above. A level marked *hidden until week N* is not offered by the app before that week even if the user would otherwise be ready for it; a level marked *gated* needs a one-time confirmation first.

### Horizontal Push

Shoulders "collapse inward" under push-up load because serratus anterior is failing to hold protraction and upward rotation, so the first four levels are scapular-control drills, not push-ups — wall slides and the push-up plus give high serratus activity with low upper-trapezius activity, and setting the scapula before the arm moves is what raises lower-trap and lowers upper-trap recruitment. This is safe territory for a hypermobile shoulder: the only heavy-strengthening trial in hypermobility spectrum disorder showed strength and symptom gains with no serious adverse events, because load is the treatment and end range is the problem.

### Pull (No Bar)

Without a bar, rings, or bands there is no way to load the back near failure at bodyweight, so this ladder front-loads floor scapular work — prone Y/T/W and reverse snow angels are the highest-value items here, not filler, matching two of the five exercises in the only heavy-strengthening trial for hypermobile shoulders. Table-edge and doorframe rows are added carefully: most flat-pack tables are not rated for a concentrated edge load, so table rows are gated behind a 30-second sit test, and isometric towel work trains three elbow angles since isometric strength gains are joint-angle specific.

### Squat

Patellofemoral joint force peaks near 90° of knee flexion and rises with depth and load, so early levels stay above roughly 60-90° flexion while the sit-to-stand pattern rebuilds the quadriceps recruitment obese beginners show is reduced during that task. Depth is earned, not avoided forever — once parallel is solid, extra depth is added gradually rather than skipped out of unfounded fear, since deep squatting is safe absent prior injury.

### Hinge

The hinge is taught with kinaesthetic constraint rather than verbal cueing — the wall-tap and the three-point towel-along-the-spine drill give instant self-correcting feedback about spinal neutral, because this is the movement pattern the user will rely on for the rest of their life. Glute bridging at a ~135° knee angle preferentially recruits glute max over hamstrings, which matters because hamstring cramping is the most common way a beginner drops out of the exercise.

### Core

Following McGill's argument, the core ladder trains the spine to resist motion and builds endurance rather than repeatedly flexing it — sit-ups and crunches are avoided, and the curl-up substitutes for them without cycling the spine. Holds are short and repeated (descending reps like 6/4/2) rather than marathon planks, which builds endurance without the form breakdown that comes from holding to fatigue; dead bug and bird dog are the anti-extension and anti-rotation entries, and plank difficulty is scaled by incline, which is also the wrist-friendliest lever.

## Warm-up (fixed, ~5 min)

Nothing here is progressed — it stays at 5 minutes permanently. If a session has to be short, cut the cool-down and the general raise before ever cutting the serratus activation or wrist prep.

| Step | Cue | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| General raise | Marching on the spot, step-touch, and arm swings — raise tissue temperature and heart rate before the session. | 120 s |
| Wrist prep | Palms-down and palms-up floor rocks (small range), fist-open cycles, gentle forearm circles. | 45 s |
| Cat-cow + open book | Cat-cow × 6 and open-book thoracic rotation × 5 per side — dynamic, mid-range spinal motion. | 60 s |
| Serratus activation | 8 wall slides plus 8 wall serratus presses — primes the muscle that fails first during pushing. | 60 s |
| Movement rehearsal | 10 glute bridges and 5 bodyweight sit-to-stands — rehearse today's patterns before loading them. | 60 s |

## Cool-down (optional, ~2 min)

| Step | Cue | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Easy walk | An easy walk around the room, nothing strenuous. | 90 s |
| Slow nasal breaths | 4–6 slow nasal breaths lying down. | 30 s |

Static stretching is deliberately absent here — it does not help DOMS or short-term recovery, and end-range stretching is the wrong habit for a hypermobile shoulder.

## Rest-day mobility (~5 min)

A fixed routine on non-training days — dynamic, mid-range movement, never static holds or end-range stretching.

| Step | Cue | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow | 6 slow cat-cow cycles for dynamic, mid-range spinal motion. | 60 s |
| Open-book rotation | 5 open-book thoracic rotations per side. | 60 s |
| Wall slides | Forearms on the wall, slide up and down keeping constant pressure — light serratus activation. | 60 s |
| Glute bridges | Slow glute bridges, squeeze the glutes at the top. | 60 s |
| Easy walk | An easy walk to finish, nothing strenuous. | 120 s |

## Safety screening (before starting)

The PAR-Q+ questions plus four app-specific items, asked once at onboarding and re-run every 12 months or after a health change:

1. Has your doctor ever said that you have a heart condition or high blood pressure? — **hard gate**
2. Do you feel pain in your chest at rest, during your daily activities of living, or when you do physical activity? — **hard gate**
3. Do you lose balance because of dizziness or have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months? — **hard gate**
4. Have you ever been diagnosed with another chronic medical condition? — soft gate (recommend clearance)
5. Are you currently taking prescribed medications for a chronic medical condition? — soft gate (recommend clearance)
6. Do you currently have — or have you had in the past 12 months — a bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that could be made worse by becoming more active? — advisory
7. Has your doctor ever said that you should only do medically supervised physical activity? — **hard gate**
8. Have you ever dislocated or subluxed a shoulder, or does a shoulder slip / feel unstable? — adapts the program: push ladder locked at incline ≥ chest height for 4 weeks
9. Do you get wrist pain, or numbness or tingling in your hands? — adapts the program: all hand-loaded work defaults to fists / forearms
10. Are you pregnant or within 6 months post-partum? — soft gate (recommend clearance)
11. Do you know your blood pressure, and was the last reading ≥ 140/90? — advisory

A hard gate blocks the program entirely with no skip button. BMI alone is never a gate.

## The six daily habits

Shown as plain checkboxes, no calorie or macro entry anywhere:

| Habit | Why |
|---|---|
| Protein at every meal (~30 g) | ≥25-30 g of protein per meal improves appetite control and body-weight outcomes during weight loss. |
| Vegetables or fruit at 2 meals | A single fibre goal of ≥30 g/day produced weight loss comparable to a far more complex 13-component diet. |
| No sugary drinks today | Adding sugar-sweetened drinks adds weight and removing them takes it back off in controlled trials. |
| A large glass of water before each main meal | Pre-meal water added roughly 2 kg of extra weight loss over 12 weeks in randomized trials. |
| 7+ hours in bed | Short sleep redirects weight loss away from fat and into muscle, and under-8-hour sleep tracks with higher injury odds. |
| Walk target hit | Daily steps are the aerobic base that ramps gradually alongside the strength work. |

## Ladder-specific safety notes

- **Push:** shoulder blades winging on any rep ends the set immediately; the next session runs one level lower. Any week the shoulder aches, spend one full session back at P3/P4 — control work is never wasted for this user.
- **Pull:** any shoulder click, pinch, or slipping sensation at the bottom of a row drops two levels back to L3–L4 for a week — hypermobile shoulders are most at risk at the bottom, fully protracted position. Table rows are gated behind a 30-second sit test on the actual table; most flat-pack tables fail it.
- **Squat:** anterior knee pain above 3/10 during or the next day returns the user to S1/S2 for two sessions, then re-entry one level below where the pain appeared. Split squats (S9/S10) are removed entirely — not just regressed — if knee pain recurs twice.
- **Hinge:** any low-back ache during or after a hinge drops the user to H6 (the towel constraint drill) regardless of current level; hamstring cramping in bridges is not a regression trigger.
- **Core:** any low-back ache sends the user back to C1–C4 for a week; any shoulder sinking or wrist pain in planks sends them back to C7/C8 (incline) with fists; no hold is ever taken past 30 s.

Full rule precedence and the adaptive engine live in `src/engine/` and [RESEARCH.md §2.3](RESEARCH.md).
