# R1 — Beginner Programs & Programming Evidence

**Target user:** untrained adult, BMI ~30–35, breathless on stairs, zero training history. Home, no equipment (floor, wall, chair/table, towel, doorframe). Shoulder hypermobility + weak scapular stabilisers (shoulders "collapse inward" in push-ups), sensitive wrists. 3×/week, 20–25 min, evenings. Quits on vagueness or shame.

**Source ratings:** `[PR]` peer-reviewed · `[G]` guideline body · `[E]` expert practitioner · `[C]` community consensus.

**Sourcing caveat:** reddit.com is not directly fetchable from this environment. r/bodyweightfitness material was read via the community's own mirrors ([gist mirror of the RR](https://gist.github.com/sgup/f10f1d57e54b7876495f4bafb6d697eb), [nick-e.com for the Primer](https://nick-e.com/primer/)) and search-indexed wiki text. Convict Conditioning is a paid book; structure taken from [public reference notes](https://gist.github.com/avar/1575165).

## 2. Per-program notes

**Hybrid Calisthenics** — closest thing to a purpose-built on-ramp. Every ladder starts below bodyweight: push-ups at the **wall**, squats at **jackknife squats**, where you "assist with our arms — even if this means assisting heavily at first" ([squats](https://www.hybridcalisthenics.com/squats) `[E]`). Progression is a published number, never a judgement call. Two mismatches: 6 days/week, and Level-3 standards that are endurance targets (3×50 wall push-ups) taking months — which reads as stagnation. Both fixable by re-mapping the ladders onto 3 full-body days with intermediate milestones.

**BWF Recommended Routine** — best-validated community program, disqualified on equipment and time (pull-up bar + dip station, ~60 min) `[C]`. Its *rules* transfer perfectly: **3×8 clean → harder variation; <3×5 → regress**, 90 s rest, antagonist supersets to save time.

**BWF Primer** — solves the exact psychological problem in the brief: **one exercise on day 1**, one element added per day, 6 exercises by day 14, ~20 min, **no equipment** ([nick-e.com](https://nick-e.com/primer/) `[C]`). Its bird dog and deadbug are also standard scapular/trunk control drills — directly on point for "shoulders collapse inward".

**Convict Conditioning** — one good idea worth stealing: **three standards per step** (e.g. wall push-up 1×10 → 2×25 → 3×50), so a win exists long before you graduate the step. Against it: uneven step jumps, unverifiable mythology, and a tone that [dismisses everyone who lifts weights](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vvf53bbgTM4uWFAuW/convict-conditioning-book-review) `[C]` — bad for someone who quits when failure feels shameful. Its bridge and handstand push-up ladders are wrong for a hypermobile, wrist-sensitive shoulder.

**Overcoming Gravity / Steven Low** — a design reference, not a program: full-body, every other day, 3×/week, six exercises, "beginners have poorer work capacity and recovery than conditioned athletes" `[E]`. Being a template, it hands the user the programming decision — the worst thing to do to someone who quits on vagueness.

**ACSM + WHO** set the floor. WHO: strength work **2+ days/week**, all major muscle groups, "start by doing small amounts of physical activity and gradually increase" `[G]`. ACSM novice: **8–12 reps, 2–3 days/week**, advance when 1–2 reps over target on two consecutive sessions `[G]`. For obesity, [Cleve Clin J Med](https://www.ccjm.org/content/83/2/141) `[PR]` is blunter — begin at **one set of 8–12, 2 days/week**; "to minimize dropout, avoid hard exercise too soon for people with a low exercise capacity or high body mass index", since self-regulated intensity "reduces the likelihood of excessive delayed-onset muscle soreness, which can discourage new participants."

**Bodyweight is not a compromise.** [Kotarsky 2018, JSCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/) `[PR]`: progressive calisthenic push-ups 3 d/wk × 4 weeks gave strength and muscle-thickness gains comparable to bench press. [Calatayud 2015, JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2015/01000/bench_press_and_push_up_at_comparable_levels_of.31.aspx) `[PR]`: equal 1RM/6RM gains over 5 weeks when push-up and bench press were activity-matched.

## 3. Programming evidence

### 3.1 Full-body 3×/week vs other splits
[Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) `[PR]` (25 studies): training a muscle **≥2×/week beats 1×/week** for hypertrophy at equated volume; 3× vs 2× undetermined. [Grgic et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/) `[PR]` lands in the same place for strength — frequency effects largely vanish once weekly volume is equated, and **2–3 d/wk is the standard finding for previously untrained people**. Full-body also means a missed day costs a third of everything rather than all of one movement.
**Rule for our app:** three identical full-body sessions per week, every other day; never a body-part split.

### 3.2 Sets × reps × rest for novices
ACSM novice: **8–12 reps, 2–3 d/wk** `[G]`; obese beginners **start at 1 set** `[PR]`. [Ralston et al. 2017](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-weekly-set-volume-on-strength-gain-a-meta-analysis/) `[PR]`: strength ES 0.82 (low weekly sets) / 0.98 (medium) / 1.01 (high) — high-vs-low difference only **0.18 (p = 0.003)**, and low weekly sets already produce strength gains in novices. [Schoenfeld et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/) `[PR]`: ~**0.37 % extra hypertrophy per added weekly set** — real but shallow. Rest: [Grgic et al. 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/953a5adf43cc59b5793958ea6a1c26d0031d1587) `[PR]` — in **untrained** people short and long inter-set rests are both viable; longer rest matters mainly for the trained.
**Rule for our app:** 2–3 sets × 8–12 reps per movement, **60–90 s rest**, and week 1 is a single set per movement.

### 3.3 RIR / RPE for beginners
[Refalo et al. 2024 meta-regression](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295) `[PR]`: proximity to failure has a **negligible relationship with strength gain** across a wide RIR range, while hypertrophy improves modestly as sets end closer to failure. [Refalo et al. 2023 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/) `[PR]` puts the failure-vs-non-failure hypertrophy edge at ES ≈ 0.15–0.21 — trivial. Beginners also estimate RIR badly, and for our user a set taken to failure is a scripted shame event.
**Rule for our app:** every set stops at **2–3 reps in reserve** ("stop when form changes or two reps feel impossible"); never prescribe training to failure.

### 3.4 Progressive overload without weights
Spend the levers in this order: **reps → sets → range of motion → tempo (slower eccentric) → pauses → leverage (incline height, limb position) → harder variation**. Hybrid and Convict make leverage the axis and reps the counter `[E]`; the RR makes variation the axis `[C]`. ACSM's load rule converts directly: advance when you exceed the target by **1–2 reps on two consecutive sessions** `[G]`. Lowering a push-up's incline in ~10 cm decrements (table → counter → chair → step → floor) is the equipment-free analogue of adding plates, and the one lever that lets a hypermobile shoulder keep scapular control while still overloading.
**Rule for our app:** one lever at a time, in that fixed order, with the incline height logged in centimetres so progress is visible even when reps don't move.

### 3.5 Grease the Groove
Pavel's method: one movement, **half the reps you can manage (never more than ~5)**, many times a day, **never to failure**, long rests `[E]` ([StrongFirst](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/greasing-the-groove.27367/)). Evidence quality is **weak-indirect** — no RCTs of GTG itself. It borrows credibility from the frequency literature (§3.1) and distributed-practice motor learning; against it, it is a neural/skill method with little hypertrophic stimulus and it presupposes a movement you can already do cleanly. Not the program — but a good *rescue* on a day the session won't happen.
**Rule for our app:** never the main program; offer it only as a 2-minute "minimum day" (one easy set of half-max reps of one movement), and treat it as habit maintenance, not training.

### 3.6 Deload
[Bell et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223/full) `[PR]`: competitive athletes deload ~**6.4 ± 1.7 days every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks** — but the same literature notes that reaching overreaching from resistance training alone is unlikely over short periods with realistic protocols. A [1-week deload during supervised RT](https://peerj.com/articles/16777/) `[PR]` did not impair, and in places improved, adaptations. A novice doing 2–3 sets at 2–3 RIR three times a week is not accumulating the fatigue a deload exists to clear.
**Rule for our app:** no scheduled deloads; instead trigger a *reactive* easy week (same sessions, one set each, one step back in progression) when the user logs two consecutive sessions of missed rep targets or flags joint soreness.

### 3.7 Missed day / missed week, and detraining
Strength is **maintained for roughly 3–4 weeks without training**, with no meaningful atrophy in beginners; week-1 "shrinking" is glycogen and water, not muscle ([detraining literature review](https://sci-fit.net/detraining/) `[E]`, consistent with [McMaster et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23629715/) `[PR]`). Real losses need ~8+ weeks, and retraining is faster than the original build. The "start over" spiral is therefore a belief problem, not a physiology one: mastery experiences are the strongest source of exercise self-efficacy, and beginners drop out through lost confidence and fear of judgement ([RCT in overweight/obese adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13496450/) `[PR]`).
**Rule for our app:** a missed session is never rescheduled or repaid — resume at the same level. After ≤3 weeks away, resume unchanged; after 3–8 weeks, repeat the last completed week; after 8+ weeks, drop one progression step. Streaks must count *weeks with ≥1 session*, never consecutive days.

### 3.8 Minimum effective dose
[Androulakis-Korakakis et al. 2020, *Sports Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31797219/) `[PR]`: in resistance-trained men, **one set of 6–12 reps at ~70–85 % 1RM, 2–3×/week for 8–12 weeks** produced significant (if sub-optimal) 1RM gains; in powerlifters, [~3–6 working sets/week per lift](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.713655/full) `[PR]` sufficed. Untrained people need less, so that is a generous upper bound on our floor. With Ralston's shallow volume gradient, a 20–25-minute session is comfortably above threshold, not a compromise.
**Rule for our app:** the advertised floor is **1 set × 8–12 reps × 5 movements, 3×/week** (~10 min); anything above that is a bonus, and the app must say so out loud.

## 4. Recommendation (ranked)

**1 — Adopt: BWF Primer's on-ramp + Hybrid Calisthenics' progression ladders, on an RR-style 3×/week full-body schedule.** Nothing off the shelf fits; every part of this hybrid is load-bearing:
- **Primer for weeks 1–2** — one exercise on day 1, six by day 14, ~20 min, no equipment. The only reviewed program that treats "has never trained" as its own state; its bird dog / deadbug / glute bridge are the scapular and trunk control work the shoulder complaint calls for.
- **Hybrid Calisthenics ladders as the exercise library** — wall → incline → knee → full push-up; jackknife → assisted → half → full squat. Published, unambiguous, bottom rungs genuinely achievable at BMI 33. Swap in a **towel/doorframe isometric row plus table row** for the pull, so nothing needs a pull-up bar.
- **RR's progression rule, not Hybrid's standards** — `3×8 clean → harder variation; <3×5 → regress`, 60–90 s rest. Hybrid's 3×50 endurance targets are too slow to feel like progress; keep Convict's three-tier marks per step so a visible win lands every 1–2 weeks.
- **Volume:** week 1 = 1 set per movement (obesity prescription), building to 2–3 × 8–12 at 2–3 RIR.

**2 — BWF Minimalist Routine.** Right length, right patterns, right progression rule; loses only on the pulling anchor and the absent on-ramp.

**3 — Hybrid Calisthenics as published.** Best library and the kindest tone, but 6 days/week with 2–3 min rests doesn't fit 3×25 min, and its standards sit too far away.

**4 — Convict Conditioning.** Take the three-tier standards; leave the tone, the uneven jumps, the bridge/HSPU ladders.

**5 — BWF Recommended Routine / Overcoming Gravity.** Good programs for the wrong person — equipment-gated and ~60 min (RR), or a self-programming template (OG). Revisit the RR as the *graduation* target in ~6 months.

**Not adopted:** Start Bodyweight (covered better by Hybrid; site unreachable for verification), GTG as a primary method (§3.5).
