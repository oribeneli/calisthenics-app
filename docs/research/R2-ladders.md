# R2 — No-Equipment Progression Ladders

**Target user:** untrained adult, BMI ~30–35, no training history. Home only: floor, wall, sturdy chair/table, towel, doorframe. **Shoulder hypermobility with weak scapular stabilizers** (shoulders "collapse inward" under push-up load = loss of serratus-driven protraction/upward rotation). **Sensitive wrists.** 3 sessions × 20–25 min/week.

**Source rating key:** `[PR]` peer-reviewed · `[G]` guideline/official body · `[E]` expert practitioner · `[C]` community consensus.

## Global rules (apply to every ladder unless a ladder overrides)

- **Advance rule (default):** hit the level's stated target **with clean form, in two consecutive sessions**, then move up. This is the ACSM "2-for-2"-style progression — advance only once the current workload is exceeded on repeated occasions, not on one good day ([ACSM Progression Models, 2009](https://tourniquets.org/wp-content/uploads/PDFs/ACSM-Progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults-2009.pdf) `[G]`).
- **Regress rule (default):** drop one level if **any** of: (a) you cannot hit the *previous* level's minimum in a session; (b) a form fault appears in ≥2 reps of a set; (c) pain >3/10 during, or >24 h of new joint pain after; (d) for pushing, the scapulae wing/collapse on any rep.
- **Volume for this user:** start at **1 set of 8–12** per exercise, build to 2–3 sets. ACSM specifically advises deconditioned/obese beginners start at *one* set of 8–12 reps, 2 days/week, low resistance, before adding sets ([ACSM via CCJM, 2016](https://www.ccjm.org/content/83/2/141) `[G]`; [ACSM 2026 Position Stand](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) `[G]` — "moving from no resistance training to any" is where the gain is; training each muscle group 2×/week beats program complexity).
- **Tempo default:** 2 s down, 1 s pause, 2 s up ([Hybrid Calisthenics](https://www.hybridcalisthenics.com/wall-pushups) `[E]`). Tempo is the cheapest progression once reps get high.
- **Never train to failure** in the first 8–12 weeks. ACSM 2026 states failure training is not required for health/fitness outcomes `[G]`; for a hypermobile shoulder, failure is exactly where scapular control is lost.

## 1. Horizontal push (12 levels)

**Why the first four levels are not push-ups.** "Shoulders collapse inward" is serratus anterior failing to hold protraction and upward rotation. Serratus-biased drills — wall slide, push-up plus, dynamic hug — give high serratus activity with *low upper-trapezius* activity, the ratio you want ([Hardwick, JOSPT 2006](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2006.2306) `[PR]`; [Ekstrom, JOSPT 2003](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2003.33.5.247) `[PR]`). The wall slide works at *and above* 90° of elevation where the push-up plus alone does not (Hardwick), so both are needed. Adding the "plus" to a knee push-up lifts serratus activation into the 40–55 %MVIC band associated with strength adaptation ([Jung, *Life* 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12734928/) `[PR]`; [knee push-up plus EMG, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8701807/) `[PR]`). Positioning the scapula consciously before the arm moves raises lower-trap and lowers upper-trap activity ([JOSPT 2013](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2013.4283) `[PR]`), and scapular stabilisation beats generic strengthening for dyskinesis ([review, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7365732/) `[PR]`; [RCT, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38432789/) `[PR]`).

| # | Level | Setup / description | Cues | Two common faults | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | `push.wall_serratus_press` | Stand arm's length from wall, palms flat at chest height, elbows **straight but not locked**. Without bending the elbows, push the chest away from the wall (blades spread), then let the chest drift back 2 cm only. | "Push the wall away"; "spread the blades, ribs down"; elbows soft, never locked | Bending the elbows (it becomes a push-up); shrugging toward the ears | 2 × 10 slow (2 s push, 2 s hold) |
| P2 | `push.wall_slide_forearms` | Forearms on wall, pinkie edges touching, elbows at chest height. Slide forearms up the wall to head height keeping constant pressure; lower under control. | Press forearms into the wall throughout; let the blades rotate up and *around* the ribs; no shrug | Losing wall contact at the top; neck jutting forward | 2 × 8 slides |
| P3 | `push.scap_wall_pushup` | P1 position plus a small elbow bend: lower the chest 10–15 cm to the wall **letting the blades come together**, then push away and **finish with an extra protraction ("plus")**. | Blades together going in, blades apart at the top; chest leads, not head | Skipping the "plus"; letting the chest sink between the shoulders at the bottom | 2 × 12 |
| P4 | `push.kneeling_scap_plus` | Hands (or fists) on a chair seat, knees on the floor, body straight knee-to-head, arms locked soft. No elbow bend at all: let the chest sink 3–5 cm between the blades, then press up and protract. | Sink only 3 cm, not to end range; ribs down; squeeze glutes | Letting the chest drop to end range (the exact collapse you are training out of); low back arching | 2 × 10 |
| P5 | `push.wall_pushup` | Feet ~60 cm from the wall, hands at chest height, shoulder-width. Lower the chest to the wall, press back, finish with a light "plus". | 2-1-2 tempo; elbows ~45° from the torso, not flared to 90°; one line ear-to-heel | Head reaching the wall before the chest; hips sagging forward | 3 × 15 → **3 × 25** |
| P6 | `push.counter_pushup` | Hands on a kitchen counter or high table edge (~95–105 cm). Same mechanics. | Grip the edge so the wrists stay nearer neutral; set the blades before the first rep | Hands wider than needed; bottom position with blades pinched and chest collapsed | 3 × 12 → **3 × 15** |
| P7 | `push.table_pushup` | Hands on a sturdy dining table (~75 cm). | Elbows track back, not out; 1 s pause at the bottom with the blades still controlled | Bouncing off the bottom; shrugging on the press | 3 × 12 → **3 × 15** |
| P8 | `push.chair_pushup` | Hands on a chair seat or sofa arm (~45 cm). Brace the chair against a wall so it cannot slide. | Same ear-to-heel line; squeeze the glutes to stop the hips sagging | Chair sliding (unsafe — stop); hips leading the descent | 3 × 12 |
| P9 | `push.knee_pushup` | Knees on a folded towel, ankles crossed, hands on fists or on the two lowest stair steps. | Body straight knee→head; nose to the floor, not forehead; small "plus" to finish each rep | Hips piking up; hands too far forward of the shoulders | 3 × 12 → **3 × 20** |
| P10 | `push.knee_pushup_plus` | As P9, but every rep ends with a deliberate 1 s end-range protraction hold. | "Press the floor away and round the upper back 2 cm"; keep the neck long | Shrugging instead of protracting; losing the knee-to-head line during the plus | 3 × 12 with 1 s holds |
| P11 | `push.full_pushup_partial` | Full plank on fists or push-up handles, lower **only to ~45° elbow bend** (chest ~25 cm off the floor), press up. Partial range keeps you out of the end range where the shoulder collapses. | Set the blades before unweighting; glutes and abs on; stop the descent before the chest "sinks between" the shoulders | Descending to the floor before control exists; low back sagging | 3 × 8 |
| P12 | `push.full_pushup` | Full push-up, chest to fist height, on fists or handles. | Elbows 45°; full protraction at lockout; whole body one rigid unit | Winging on the last 2 reps of a set (end the set there); elbows flared to 90° | 3 × 8 → **3 × 15** |

**Advance rule:** move up one level when you hit the bolded (or single) target **with zero winging and zero wrist pain, in two consecutive sessions**. For P1–P4 (control levels) advance when the target reps are done with a visible, symmetrical "plus" on every rep. Practitioner standards are much higher — Hybrid Calisthenics gates inclines behind 3 × 50 wall push-ups `[E]`, and Convict Conditioning's kneeling push-up progression standard is 3 × 30 `[E]` — but those are long-term aspirations, not necessary gates: 3 × 15–25 is ample mechanical tension for a novice and reaches the next level far sooner.

**Regress rule:** the default rule, plus one push-specific trigger — **if the shoulder blades wing on any single rep, that set ends immediately and the next session runs one level lower.** Also drop to P3/P4 for one full session in any week the shoulder aches; control work is never wasted volume for this user.

## 2. Pull with no bar (10 levels) — and an honest ceiling

**Honest assessment first.** Without a bar, rings, or bands there is **no way to load the back near failure at 100+ kg bodyweight**. The r/bodyweightfitness FAQ's own no-bar answers — rows under a table, a stick across two chairs, a bedsheet on a closed door — are workarounds, not equals ([r/bwf row progression](https://asdjflk.github.io/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/exercises/row.html) `[C]`; [r/bwf FAQ](https://asdjflk.github.io/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/faq.html) `[C]`).

Specifically, for this user:

- **Table-edge rows: assess before use.** A row puts a levered, partly *upward* load on one table edge; most flat-pack tables are rated for static distributed load, not ~60–80 kg concentrated at an edge with movement. **Rule: solid-wood or steel-frame only; sit on the edge for 30 s first — if it flexes, creaks, or tips, do not row from it.** A very heavy beginner often fails this test, which is why this ladder front-loads floor work.
- **Towel–doorframe rows are genuinely useful** and self-limiting: foot position sets the load, and the frame takes a pulling load in line. Use the **hinge side**, door open and wedged, towel around the door edge below the handle or the frame upright.
- **Prone Y/T/W and reverse snow angels are the highest-value items here, not filler.** Prone Y is the gold-standard lower-trapezius exercise (~75 %MVC) and prone horizontal abduction tops the middle-trapezius list ([Ekstrom, JOSPT 2003](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2003.33.5.247) `[PR]`; [prone-Y depression strategy, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390048884_Effectiveness_of_the_scapular_depression_strategy_to_activate_the_lower_trapezius_during_prone_Y_exercise) `[PR]`). Two of the five exercises in the only heavy-strengthening trial in hypermobility spectrum disorder are prone horizontal abduction and prone ER at 90° ([Liaghat et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7350677/) `[PR]`).
- **Isometric towel pulls work, but narrowly.** Isometric gains are *joint-angle specific*, carrying over only ~±15–20° around the trained angle ([Lanza et al., 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-019-04229-z) `[PR]`) — so train 2–3 elbow angles, never one.

**Ceiling:** roughly **a controlled 3 × 8 horizontal towel row, feet far forward (torso near 45° or lower)**. Past that, progress needs a doorway bar, rings, or bands (~$20–30). Plan that purchase for month 3; this ladder makes it earnable, not unnecessary.

| # | Level | Setup / description | Cues | Two common faults | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | `pull.prone_scap_retract` | Face down on the floor, arms by the sides, forehead on a towel. Squeeze the blades down and together, hold, release. No arm lift. | "Blades into your back pockets"; chin tucked; keep breathing | Shrugging up instead of back-and-down; lifting the chest | 3 × 8 × 5 s holds |
| L2 | `pull.prone_t` | Prone, arms straight out sideways (T), thumbs up. Lift the arms ~5 cm, hold 3 s, lower. | Lead with the blade, not the hand; ribs stay on the floor | Arching the low back to lift higher; shrugging | 2 × 10 × 3 s |
| L3 | `pull.prone_y` | Prone, arms overhead at ~120° (Y), thumbs up. Retract **and depress** the blades first, then lift the arms. | Depress first, then lift — that is what recruits lower trap; keep the neck long | Starting the lift from the hand; upper traps taking over | 2 × 10 × 3 s |
| L4 | `pull.reverse_snow_angel` | Prone, palms down, arms by the sides. Slide the arms along the floor out to overhead and back, keeping light floor contact throughout. | Keep contact the whole arc; go only as high as contact allows; slow | Losing floor contact mid-arc; bending the elbows to fake range | 2 × 6 slow arcs |
| L5 | `pull.isometric_towel_row` | Seated or standing, towel around a doorframe upright (or gripped in both hands and pulled apart). Pull into a row position and hold. Use three angles: arms long, elbows 90°, elbows fully back. | Ramp to ~70% effort over 2 s; blades back first, then elbows; exhale through the hold | Holding the breath / bearing down; pulling with the biceps only | 3 angles × 2 × 10 s |
| L6 | `pull.bent_over_towel_row` | Hinge at the hips to ~45°, towel under both feet, grip the ends, pull the elbows back against your own resistance. | Neutral spine, chest proud; elbows brush the ribs; resist the lowering too | Rounding the low back; jerking | 3 × 10 with 2 s eccentric |
| L7 | `pull.doorframe_row_high` | Towel around a door edge on the **hinge side**, door open and wedged. Grip both ends, feet close to the door, lean back only ~20°. Pull the chest toward the hands. | Straight line ankle→head; blades back before the elbows bend; ribs down | Hips sagging (it becomes a squat-row); yanking the door | 3 × 10 |
| L8 | `pull.doorframe_row_mid` | As L7, feet walked forward so the torso leans back ~35°. | Same cues; 1 s pause at the top with the chest near the hands | Shrugging at the top; shoulders rolling forward at the bottom | 3 × 8 |
| L9 | `pull.table_row_bent_knee` | **Only if the table passed the sit-test.** Lie under the table, grip the edge, knees bent, feet flat. Pull the chest to the edge. | Bent knees cut the load; squeeze the glutes; elbows ~45° | Table tipping (stop immediately); pulling with the neck | 3 × 8 |
| L10 | `pull.doorframe_row_low` / `pull.table_row_straight_leg` | Feet walked far forward, torso toward horizontal; or table row with legs straight, heels on the floor. | Rigid body; 2 s down; full retraction at the top | Hips piking; partial range | 3 × 8 — **ceiling; buy bands or a doorway bar here** |

**Advance rule:** 3 × 8 clean reps (or the stated holds) in two consecutive sessions — the r/bwf 3 × 8 convention `[C]`. On L1–L4, advance only when the reps are done with no shrug and no low-back arch, not merely when the count is reached.

**Regress rule:** the default rule, plus a pull-specific trigger — **any shoulder click, pinch, or "slipping" sensation at the bottom of a row drops you two levels and back to L3–L4 for a week.** Hypermobile shoulders are most at risk at the bottom position (arms long, fully protracted): never let the shoulder hang passively there, keep light tension.

## 3. Squat (10 levels)

**Knee-loading concerns for an obese beginner.** Patellofemoral joint force and stress peak around **90° of knee flexion** and rise with depth and external load ([Wallace et al., JOSPT 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11949662/) `[PR]`), so the early levels stay above 90° (mini-squat / high box, ~40–60° flexion). That is a *dose* argument, not a safety scare: a scoping review found 87% of studies affirm deep squatting is safe absent prior injury ([2024 scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11618833/) `[PR]`; [Physio Network](https://www.physio-network.com/blog/is-it-safe-to-squat-deep-what-does-the-evidence-say/) `[E]`). Depth is earned, not avoided forever. Sit-to-stand is the right entry point: repeated chair rises measurably increase knee-extensor strength and reduce the muscular effort of the task in deconditioned people ([sit-to-stand in frail elders](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0531556518307058) `[PR]`), and obese women show *reduced* voluntary quadriceps recruitment during sit-to-stand — a deficit functional training targets directly ([Physical Therapy, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/ptj/article/100/12/2134/5906047) `[PR]`).

| # | Level | Setup / description | Cues | Two common faults | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | `squat.wall_sit_shallow` | Back flat on the wall, feet forward, slide down to only ~30–40° knee bend. Hold. | Even pressure through the whole foot; knees over mid-foot; breathe normally | Sliding too deep too soon; holding the breath | 3 × 20–30 s |
| S2 | `squat.sit_to_stand_high` | Chair with a cushion (or the arm of a sofa) so the hips sit well above the knees. Stand and sit, hands free if possible, on the chair arms if not. | Nose over toes on the way up; push the floor away; sit down *slowly* (3 s) | Flopping into the seat; using the hands to pull up once you no longer need to | 3 × 8 → **3 × 12** |
| S3 | `squat.sit_to_stand_normal` | Standard dining chair, no hands. | 3 s down, touch, 1 s pause, stand; knees track over the 2nd toe | Rocking the torso forward for momentum; knees collapsing inward | 3 × 10 → **3 × 15** |
| S4 | `squat.box_squat_high` | Same chair, but **tap** the seat and stand without sitting — continuous tension. | Hips back first, then the knees bend; chest up; tap, don't rest | Sitting down fully (that is S3); heels lifting | 3 × 10 |
| S5 | `squat.box_squat_low` | Lower target (low stool or a stack of books, ~35 cm) — approaching 90°. | Same cues; control the last 5 cm | Dropping the last 5 cm; knees caving | 3 × 10 |
| S6 | `squat.doorframe_assisted` | Face a doorframe, hold both uprights lightly, squat as deep as is comfortable, using the hands for balance and ~10% assistance only. | Hands are a balance aid, not a winch; sit *between* the heels; heels down | Pulling yourself up with the arms; leaning back into the hands | 3 × 10 |
| S7 | `squat.bodyweight_parallel` | Free squat to thighs-parallel, feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. | Screw the feet into the floor; knees out; brace the abs before descending | Butt-wink (low back rounding at the bottom — stop above that depth); heels rising | 3 × 10 → **3 × 15** |
| S8 | `squat.tempo_squat` | Parallel squat: 4 s down, 2 s pause at the bottom, 1 s up. | Stay tight in the pause; no bouncing out of the hole | Shortening the pause as you fatigue; losing the brace | 3 × 8 |
| S9 | `squat.split_squat_assisted` | Staggered stance, rear foot flat on the floor behind (not elevated), fingertips on a wall or chair back. Lower the rear knee toward the floor. | 70% of the weight on the front foot; torso upright; a longer step is more glute-biased and knee-friendlier than a short one | Front knee travelling far past the toes with a short step; wobbling (add fingertip support rather than cutting range) | 3 × 8 per leg |
| S10 | `squat.split_squat_free` | Same, unsupported, with a 2 s pause at the bottom. | Vertical shin on the front leg; rear-knee tap, no crash | Front knee collapsing inward; leaning toward the supported side | 3 × 10 per leg |

**Advance rule:** hit the bolded (or single) target with heels down, knees tracking, and no low-back rounding, **two sessions running**. Depth is a separate axis: once S7 is solid at parallel, add ~2 cm of depth per week rather than reps, stopping at the first sign of butt-wink.

**Regress rule:** the default rule, plus: **anterior knee pain above 3/10 during or the day after → return to the last level that kept knee flexion under ~60° (S1/S2) for two sessions**, then re-enter one level below where the pain appeared. Split squats (S9/S10) are removed entirely, not regressed, if knee pain recurs twice.

## 4. Hinge (9 levels)

The hinge is the pattern most worth teaching well, because it is how this user will pick things up for the rest of their life. Teach it with **kinaesthetic constraint rather than verbal instruction**: the wall-tap and the three-point towel/dowel-along-the-spine drill give instant self-correcting feedback about spinal neutral ([The Barbell Physio](https://thebarbellphysio.com/hip-hinge-testing-fixes/) `[E]`; [Prehab Guys, single-leg RDL](https://theprehabguys.com/single-leg-romanian-deadlift/) `[E]`). Gluteus maximus activity is highest in single-leg bridging on a stable surface, and a modified bridge with the knee at ~135° rather than 90° preferentially recruits glute max over the hamstrings ([Lehecka et al., IJSPT 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319714105_BUILDING_A_BETTER_GLUTEAL_BRIDGE_ELECTROMYOGRAPHIC_ANALYSIS_OF_HIP_MUSCLE_ACTIVITY_DURING_MODIFIED_SINGLE-LEG_BRIDGES) `[PR]`) — which matters here, because hamstring-dominant bridging is the most common way a beginner cramps out of the exercise.

| # | Level | Setup / description | Cues | Two common faults | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | `hinge.glute_bridge` | On your back, knees bent ~90°, feet flat. Squeeze the glutes and lift the hips to a straight knee–hip–shoulder line. | Tuck the pelvis *before* lifting; push through the heels; ribs down | Arching the low back to gain height; hamstring cramp (move the feet closer) | 3 × 10 with 2 s holds |
| H2 | `hinge.glute_bridge_135` | As H1 but with the feet further from the hips (knee ~135°). More glute, less hamstring. | Drive through the whole foot; 3 s hold at the top | Pushing through the toes; hips dropping between reps | 3 × 12 × 3 s |
| H3 | `hinge.bridge_march` | H1 position, hips up, alternately lift one foot 5 cm. | Hips stay perfectly level; brace the abs | Hips rocking side to side; hips sinking as you march | 3 × 8 per side |
| H4 | `hinge.single_leg_bridge` | One foot planted (knee ~135°), the other knee hugged loosely or the leg straight. Lift the hips. | Level pelvis; squeeze the *working* glute; 2 s up, 2 s down | Rotating the pelvis toward the free leg; pushing off with the free leg | 3 × 8 per side |
| H5 | `hinge.wall_hip_tap` | Stand ~15 cm from a wall, feet hip-width. Push the hips straight back to tap the wall with the glutes, chest folding forward, knees only slightly bent. Stand tall. | "Shut a car door with your backside"; shins near-vertical; chest down, not chin down | Squatting down instead of pushing back; rounding the upper back | 3 × 12 |
| H6 | `hinge.towel_spine_hinge` | Hold a rolled towel or broom vertically along the spine, touching head, upper back and tailbone. Hinge while keeping all three contacts. | If a contact breaks you rounded — reset; go only as low as neutral holds | Losing head contact (chin poke); losing tailbone contact (low-back rounding) | 3 × 10 |
| H7 | `hinge.good_morning_bw` | Hands behind the head, feet hip-width, hinge to a ~45–60° torso angle, return. | Long spine; feel a stretch in the hamstrings, not the low back; squeeze the glutes to stand | Bending the knees excessively; hyperextending at the top (a hypermobility trap) | 3 × 12 |
| H8 | `hinge.staggered_rdl` | Hinge with one foot back on the toes (kickstand), ~80% of the weight on the front leg, fingertips on a chair for balance. | Hips square; hinge back, not down; front shin vertical | Letting the hip rotate open; twisting the torso | 3 × 8 per side |
| H9 | `hinge.single_leg_rdl` | Full single-leg RDL, free leg extending behind as the torso folds; fingertips on a wall at first, then unsupported. | Back leg and torso move as one lever; square the hips; 3 s down | Opening the hip to the ceiling; rounding at the bottom of the range | 3 × 8 per side, 3 s eccentric |

**Advance rule:** the stated target with the three-point towel contact intact (H6+) or a level pelvis (H3–H4, H8–H9) on **every** rep, two sessions running. For H1–H4, add hold time (2 s → 3 s → 5 s) before adding reps.

**Regress rule:** the default rule, plus: **any low-back ache during or after a hinge drops you to H6 (the towel constraint) regardless of where you were** — the fault is almost always lost neutral, and the constraint drill fixes it faster than cutting reps does. Hamstring cramping in bridges is not a regression trigger: move the feet and continue.

## 5. Core (11 levels)

McGill's argument, and the basis of this ladder: train the core to **resist** motion and build endurance rather than repeatedly flexing the spine. Repeated loaded spinal flexion (sit-ups, crunches) is the pattern to avoid; the curl-up substitutes for it without cycling the spine; and holds should be **short and repeated (~8–10 s for several reps) rather than marathon planks**, descending in reps (e.g. 6/4/2) to build endurance without fatigue-driven form loss ([Squat University on the McGill Big 3](https://squatuniversity.com/2018/06/21/the-mcgill-big-3-for-core-stability/) `[E]`; [PT Progress](https://www.ptprogress.com/big-3-exercises-for-back-pain/) `[E]`). Dead bug and bird dog are the anti-extension and anti-rotation entries; plank work is scaled by *incline*, which is also the wrist-friendliest lever.

| # | Level | Setup / description | Cues | Two common faults | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | `core.dead_bug_hands` | On your back, knees and hips at 90°, hands pressing into the knees. Press and hold, breathing. | Flatten the low back into the floor and keep it there; exhale slowly | Low back lifting off the floor; holding the breath | 3 × 20 s |
| C2 | `core.dead_bug_heel_tap` | Same, lower one heel to tap the floor, return. | Back stays flat throughout; move slowly; tap, don't drop | Ribs flaring; back arching as the leg lowers | 3 × 8 per side |
| C3 | `core.dead_bug_full` | Opposite arm and leg extend together. | Exhale as the limbs extend; go only as far as a flat back allows | Extending past control; rushing | 3 × 8 per side |
| C4 | `core.mcgill_curl_up` | On your back, one knee bent one straight, hands under the low back to preserve its natural arch. Lift head and shoulders **a few cm only**, chin tucked, no spinal flexion. | Head and shoulders move as one block; the hands keep the low-back arch; 10 s per rep | Pulling on the neck; curling into a crunch | 6/4/2 reps × 10 s |
| C5 | `core.bird_dog_arm` | Quadruped (hands on fists to spare the wrists). Extend one arm only, hold. | Hips and shoulders level; long from head to tailbone; brace lightly | Hips rotating; sagging low back | 3 × 6 per side × 8 s |
| C6 | `core.bird_dog_full` | Opposite arm and leg, hold, then sweep the limbs back under the body to draw a square. | Balance a glass on the low back; reach *long*, don't lift high | Lifting the leg above hip height (hyperextension — a hypermobility trap); shrugging the support arm | 3 × 6 per side × 8 s |
| C7 | `core.plank_wall` | Forearms on a wall, feet back ~60 cm, body straight. Hold. | Blades spread (press the wall away); tuck the pelvis; glutes on | Hips sagging; head dropping | 3 × 20–30 s |
| C8 | `core.plank_incline` | Forearms on a table or chair seat, feet on the floor. | Same; push the surface away to keep the blades spread | Shoulders drifting behind the elbows; low back arching | 3 × 20–30 s |
| C9 | `core.plank_knee` | Forearms on the floor, knees down on a towel. | Elbows under the shoulders; ribs down; squeeze the glutes; protract slightly | Sinking between the shoulders (the collapse pattern — abort the set); hips in the air | 3 × 20–30 s |
| C10 | `core.plank_full` | Full forearm plank. | One rigid line; brace as if expecting a punch; short holds, more reps | Holding to failure and sagging; head craned up | 5 × 15 s → 3 × 30 s |
| C11 | `core.side_plank_ladder` | Knees-bent side plank → full side plank → side plank with the top leg lifted. Forearm down, elbow under the shoulder. | Stack shoulder over elbow; push the floor away (don't sink into the shoulder); hips high | Sinking into the bottom shoulder — particularly risky with a lax shoulder; rolling forward | 6/4/2 reps × 10 s per side |

**Advance rule:** hold the target *without* the named faults, two sessions running; then add **reps of the same short hold**, not longer holds, until the rep target is met, and only then move up a level. Cap any single hold at 30 s.

**Regress rule:** the default rule, plus two specifics — **(a) any low-back ache → back to C1–C4 for a week; (b) any shoulder sinking or wrist pain in planks → back to C7/C8 (incline) and use fists.** Never chase a long-plank personal record.

## 6. Warm-up (5 min) and cool-down — what matters, what doesn't

**What matters (5 min, in this order):**

1. **2 min general raise** — marching on the spot, step-touch, arm swings. Raises tissue temperature and heart rate: the "R" of RAMP ([TeamBuildr on RAMP](https://blog.teambuildr.com/understanding-and-implementing-the-ramp-protocol) `[E]`).
2. **Wrist prep, 45 s** — palms-down and palms-up floor rocks (small range), fist–open cycles, gentle forearm circles. Justified by load, not ritual: push-ups on an extended wrist significantly raise radioscaphoid peak pressure versus a neutral wrist ([cadaveric study, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29157783/) `[PR]`; [J Hand Ther force-transmission study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0894113017301217) `[PR]`).
3. **Cat–cow × 6 and open-book thoracic rotation × 5/side, 60 s** — dynamic, mid-range spinal motion before hinge and push work.
4. **Serratus/scapular activation, 60 s** — 8 wall slides plus 8 wall serratus presses (P1/P2). This is the part of the warm-up that actually changes anything for this user: it primes the muscle that fails during the session ([Hardwick, JOSPT 2006](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2006.2306) `[PR]`).
5. **10 glute bridges and 5 bodyweight sit-to-stands, 60 s** — movement-specific rehearsal of the day's patterns: the "P" (potentiate) of RAMP.

**What is pointless or counterproductive here:**

- **Long static stretching before training.** Holds beyond ~60 s per muscle–tendon unit impair subsequent performance, and dynamic warm-up beats static stretching for strength and proprioceptive accuracy ([crossover RCT in shoulder impingement, 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12751158/) `[PR]`). For a hypermobile person the case is stronger still: they do not lack range, and end-range stretching feeds the instability.
- **Cool-down stretching for soreness or recovery.** A Cochrane review and a 2021 meta-analysis both find no clinically important effect of stretching on DOMS or on short-term strength/ROM recovery ([Herbert et al., Cochrane 2011](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004577.pub3/abstract) `[PR]`; [Afonso et al., Front Physiol 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.677581/full) `[PR]`). Keep the cool-down to **2 min of easy walking plus 4–6 slow nasal breaths lying down** — honest, cheap, and not oversold.
- **Shoulder CARs as a required item.** CARs produce short-term ROM changes comparable to — not better than — static stretching ([2025 comparison trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13405672/) `[PR]`), and their distinguishing feature is deliberate *end-range* work, which is the one thing this user should be doing least. If kept at all, keep shoulder circles small and mid-range.

**Advance/regress for the warm-up:** there is nothing to progress. Keep it at 5 min permanently. If a session is short, cut the cool-down and the general raise — never the serratus activation or the wrist prep.

## Hypermobility & wrist adaptations — cues and exclusions

**Core principles.** Hypermobile joints need **mid-range, closed-chain, controlled** loading: no locking out, no hyperextension, no bouncing into end range. Proprioception is impaired and the body *seeks* end range for feedback — exactly the habit to break ([EDS Society: PT for hypermobility](https://www.ehlers-danlos.org/information/physical-therapy-for-hypermobility/) `[G]`; [EDS Society: exercise for hEDS/HSD](https://www.ehlers-danlos.org/information/exercise-and-movement-for-adults-with-hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome-and-hypermobility-spectrum-disorders/) `[G]`). Strengthening is not contraindicated: the only trial of *heavy* shoulder strengthening in hypermobility spectrum disorder (5 exercises, 3×/week, 16 weeks, 3 × 10RM → 4 × 8RM) reported 100% retention, no serious adverse events, 51% symptom improvement and 28–31% strength gains ([Liaghat 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7350677/) `[PR]`; [secondary analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12997522/) `[PR]`). Load is the treatment; **end range is the problem.**

**The three rules that matter most**

1. **Set the scapula before the limb moves — every rep.** Conscious pre-positioning raises lower-trap and lowers upper-trap activity ([JOSPT 2013](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2013.4283) `[PR]`). If you cannot feel the blades set, you are not ready for that level.
2. **Stop the range short of collapse.** Never let the chest sink *between* the shoulders at the bottom of a push, and never let the shoulder hang passively at the bottom of a row. The moment the blades wing, the set is over — that rep, not the tenth rep, is the injury.
3. **Never lock out.** Elbows and knees finish "straight but soft". Finish pushes with a deliberate protraction ("plus"), which is muscular end-range, not passive joint end-range.

**Other adaptations**

- Work **closed-chain first** (hands or feet fixed: wall, floor, table). Eight weeks of closed-chain plus balance work improved proprioception, pain, strength and quality of life in hEDS `[G]`.
- **Isometric holds and slow eccentrics (2–4 s) over speed.** No bouncing, no ballistic reps, ever.
- **Tight-fitting clothing or a light compression sleeve** can improve positional awareness during sessions `[G]`.
- **Symmetry check each session:** if one side wings and the other does not, both sides train at the weaker side's level.
- Some muscle soreness is fine; **a "stuck", clunking, or slipping shoulder is not** — that was among the minor adverse events reported in the HSD trial, and is the signal to regress two levels.

**Wrist adaptations**

- **Default to a neutral wrist**: fists on a folded towel, or push-up handles / the edge of a thick book, for all floor pushing and for quadruped work (bird dog on fists). A hyperextended wrist drives load dorsally and ulnarly through ligament; a neutral wrist sends it through bone and joint ([cadaveric study, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29157783/) `[PR]`; [J Hand Ther](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0894113017301217) `[PR]`).
- **Elevate the hands** (wall → counter → table → chair). Elevation reduces both the load and the wrist extension required — the single best wrist intervention, and already the spine of the push ladder.
- **Fingers turned out slightly (10–20°)** and weight spread across the whole palm rather than the heel of the hand, if palms-down is used at all.
- **Exclusions for this user:** no straight-arm floor planks on the palms; no knuckle work on hard floors without a towel; no wrist-loaded end-range positions (downward dog, table-top wrist stretches under bodyweight); no plank hold taken to failure.

**Exercises excluded from all six ladders for this user:** behind-the-neck or overhead end-range pressing; dips; deep chest-to-floor push-ups until P12 is solid; passive hanging; any bouncing or plyometric push-up; sit-ups and crunches; loaded spinal flexion; unsupported single-leg balance work before H8; and anything performed to failure in the first 12 weeks.
