# R3 — Safety & Adaptive Rules

**User profile this is written for:** untrained adult, BMI ~30–35, no training history, breathless on stairs, home/no equipment, 3 × 20–25 min/week (evenings), shoulder hypermobility with weak scapular stabilisers, sensitive wrists, quits when failure feels shameful.

**Evidence rating key:** [PR] peer-reviewed study/review · [G] guideline / official body · [E] expert or clinician practice · [C] community/coaching convention (no formal evidence).

---

# PART A — Overweight-specific safety

## A1. Joint loading in obese beginners

**Knee (patellofemoral).** Song et al. ranked patellofemoral joint (PFJ) loading across 35 weight-bearing tasks: walking ≈ **0.6 ± 0.3 × body weight**, double-leg squat to 60° ≈ **2.5 BW**, full-depth double-leg squat ≈ **4.5 BW**, forward lunge ≈ **5.1 BW**, single-leg squat (full depth) ≈ **6.9 BW**, single-leg decline squat ≈ **8.2 BW** — "exercises with high knee flexion tended to generate higher patellofemoral joint loading" ([Song et al., AJSM 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10315869/)) [PR]. Because PFJ load scales with body weight, a 100 kg beginner in a forward lunge sees roughly the absolute joint force a 60 kg person sees in a single-leg decline squat. Obesity independently drives abnormal knee loading, malalignment and muscle weakness in the OA pathway ([J Orthop Translat 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214031X2030067X)) [PR]; a 5-unit BMI increase carries ~35% higher knee OA risk ([J Rheumatol 2024 narrative review](https://www.jrheum.org/content/51/3/224)) [PR].

**Injury risk is driven by BMI, not by exercise dose.** In an 18-month RCT of overweight/obese adults randomised to 150 / 200 / 300 min·wk⁻¹, exercise dose did **not** predict injury (p ≥ 0.41), but BMI did: OR **1.10 per BMI unit** (95% CI 1.05–1.16), obese vs. normal-weight OR **1.89** (1.06–3.33); obese participants were injured ~2.6 months earlier than overweight ones. Lower-body injuries were 21% of all events, a third of those at the knee ([Janney & Jakicic 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2818622/)) [PR]. **Implication: do not cut frequency out of fear — cut per-rep joint load.**

**Wrist.** A high plank / push-up position demands ~60–90° of wrist extension. Direct measurement showed force transmission through the wrist differs markedly between a hyperextended and a neutral wrist during push-ups, the hyperextended position concentrating dorsal radiocarpal compression and narrowing the carpal tunnel ([Clin Biomech 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0894113017301217)) [PR]. Load through the wrist is proportional to the fraction of body mass over the hands — so **incline angle is the wrist dose knob**, not only the chest dose knob.

**Shoulder (hypermobility + weak scapular stabilisers).** With lax passive restraints the active system must do the stabilising, so the rehab consensus is **mid-range control before range, stability before load**, avoiding aggressive end-range stretching ([NASM clinical guidance](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/recovery/joint-hypermobility-the-shoulder)) [E]. RCTs support loading rather than avoidance: progressive high-load strengthening improved the hypermobile shoulder ([Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003999324003319)) [PR], and home-based exercise therapy reduced instability in hEDS/HSD ([Disabil Rehabil 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09638288.2022.2076932)) [PR].

*What to build:* serratus anterior. Meta-analysis confirms the **push-up plus** produces high serratus anterior activation with comparatively low upper-trapezius activation, and the **modified (knee/incline) push-up plus** achieves serratus activation equivalent to the full version at lower load ([J Athl Train 2019 SR/MA](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/attr/54/11/article-p1156.xml)) [PR]. **Wall slides** are a high-serratus, low-load entry point ([JOSPT 2006](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2006.2306)) [PR]. Critically, in people **with scapular dyskinesis** an unstable surface *increases* trapezius and *decreases* serratus activity — train scapular work on a **stable** surface ([Man Ther 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1050641114001060)) [PR].

### Rules for our app
- Cap knee flexion at ~60° for weeks 1–8 (box/chair squat to a controllable seat height); progress depth only after pain-free volume tolerance. Rationale: ~2.5 BW vs ~4.5 BW PFJ load.
- **Blocked weeks 1–8:** jumping/landing, burpees, forward lunges and split squats (≥5 BW PFJ), single-leg squats (6.9–8.2 BW), full floor push-ups, dips, deep push-ups below chest-to-hand level, any end-range shoulder stretch, end-range overhead pressing.
- **Push chain starts at wall push-up:** wall → high incline (counter) → low incline (chair/sofa) → knee → floor. One knob (incline height) controls wrist load and shoulder load together.
- **Wrist prep is mandatory and gated:** 60–90 s of wrist flexion/extension rocking plus fist-supported holds before any hand-loaded set; offer a **fists / forearms / vertical-surface** variant of every hand-loaded exercise permanently, not as a "failure" option.
- Scapular work (wall slides, scapular push-up / push-up plus at incline) is a **separate prerequisite block**, stable surface only, 2–3×/week, completed before the push progression is allowed to advance.
- Never program the shoulder to end range. Cue: "shoulders away from ears, ribs down, soft elbows — stop 10° before the joint feels loose."

## A2. Warm-up and cool-down — what actually works

- **Static stretching before training does not prevent injury.** In a systematic review, all four RCTs found static stretching ineffective at reducing exercise-related injury ([Small et al., Res Sports Med 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18785063/)) [PR]. It also acutely reduces force output ([SR with multilevel meta-analysis, J Sport Health Sci 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095254624000693)) [PR].
- **Dynamic warm-ups are the supported option** for ROM, neuromuscular readiness and injury-risk reduction ([Arthrosc Sports Med Rehabil 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12034053/)) [PR]; a RAMP-style structure (Raise–Activate–Mobilise–Potentiate) beat static stretching and no warm-up ([Front Physiol 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12234454/)) [PR].
- **Ramp-up sets** are the ACSM-endorsed way to introduce load gradually in novices (slow/moderate velocity, 8–12 RM equivalent, whole body 2–3 d·wk⁻¹) ([ACSM Position Stand, MSSE 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)) [G].
- **Cool-downs are largely ceremonial.** An active cool-down is "largely ineffective" for same-day/next-day performance, does **not** prevent injury, and does not blunt adaptation ([Van Hooren & Peake, Sports Med 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29663142/)) [PR].

### Rules for our app
- Warm-up = fixed 3–4 min RAMP block: 60–90 s raise (march / step-touch), wrist prep, scapular activation (wall slides ×8), then **one ramp-up set at the level below today's working level** for the first exercise of each pattern.
- **Never show static stretching before a session.** Offer it only post-session, framed as optional comfort, never as injury prevention.
- Cool-down = optional 2 min breathing/walk, labelled "wind-down", explicitly **not** required for the session to count. Do not claim it prevents soreness.

## A3. Intensity ceilings with no aerobic base

- **Resistance intensity:** RPE 6/10 is a validated starting intensity for untrained people ([RCT, prescribing intensity by rating of perceived effort](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9099373/)) [PR]. Novices systematically misjudge proximity to failure — underestimating reps remaining by ~4–5 when they believe they are close ([Zourdos et al., RIR scale](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve/)) [PR]/[E]. Training to failure is not required: benefit plateaus past ~2 RIR ([meta-regression, Sports Med 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/); [failure vs non-failure SR/MA, J Sport Health Sci 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497853/)) [PR].
- **Aerobic intensity:** the talk test tracks ventilatory threshold closely ([Persinger et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15354048/)) [PR], and has been validated specifically in inactive overweight/obese adults (BMI 30.8 ± 5.0): talk difficulty **VAS 2–3/10 = VT1**, **VAS 6–7 ("hard to talk") = VT2** ([Sports Med Open 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8017038/)) [PR]. Heart-rate targets are the weaker tool here (no device, unknown HRmax, possible medication effects).
- **Session-terminating signs** mirror the absolute indications for stopping exercise: chest pain/pressure, dizziness / near-syncope / ataxia, pallor or cold clammy skin, cyanosis, nausea, disproportionate dyspnoea, unusual fatigue ([AHA Exercise Standards, *Circulation*](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hc3901.095960)) [G].

### Rules for our app
- Weeks 1–2: every working set at **RPE 5–6 / RIR 4+**. Weeks 3–5: RPE 6–7 / RIR 3. Weeks 6–8: RPE 7 / RIR 2–3. **Never prescribe RPE ≥ 9 or "to failure" in the first 8 weeks.**
- Because novice RIR is unreliable, anchor on the observable: "stop the set when the last rep looks different from the first" (tempo slows, shape changes). Log **form-break**, not RIR, as the primary stop signal.
- Conditioning ceiling = "can speak a full sentence" (VT1). If the user cannot speak a sentence, the app slows the exercise — it does not congratulate.
- Hard-stop screen, shown before the first session and reachable from any screen: **stop now and seek care** for chest pain/pressure, dizziness or feeling faint, cold sweat/pallor, nausea with breathlessness, severe shortness of breath out of proportion to effort, or new one-sided weakness. Non-dismissable content, not a toast.

## A4. Pre-participation screening

The **PAR-Q+** is the evidence-based screen; ~1% of completers need physician review, and the tool was designed to *reduce* unnecessary barriers ([PAR-Q+ 2020 form, eparmedx.com](https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/January2020PARQPlusFillable.pdf)) [G]. The **2015 ACSM algorithm** (Riebe et al.) replaced risk-factor counting with three inputs — current activity level, known cardiovascular / metabolic / renal disease or its signs and symptoms, and desired intensity — giving *no clearance needed / clearance before vigorous only / clearance before any exercise* ([MSSE 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26473759/); [applied to NHANES](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059860/)) [G]/[PR]. Our user is **inactive**, so under that algorithm *any* known CVD/metabolic/renal disease or suggestive symptom means medical clearance before starting.

**Ask exactly these (PAR-Q+ page 1, plus four app-specific items):**

1. Has your doctor ever said that you have a heart condition **or** high blood pressure?
2. Do you feel pain in your chest at rest, during your daily activities of living, **or** when you do physical activity?
3. Do you lose balance because of dizziness **or** have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months? *(answer NO if the dizziness was from over-breathing during vigorous exercise)*
4. Have you ever been diagnosed with another chronic medical condition (other than heart disease or high blood pressure)? *(list it)*
5. Are you currently taking prescribed medications for a chronic medical condition? *(list them)*
6. Do you currently have — or have you had in the past 12 months — a bone, joint or soft-tissue (muscle, ligament, tendon) problem that could be made worse by becoming more physically active? *(answer NO if it no longer limits you)*
7. Has your doctor ever said that you should only do medically supervised physical activity?
8. *(app)* Have you ever dislocated or subluxed a shoulder, or does a shoulder slip / feel unstable?
9. *(app)* Do you get wrist pain, or numbness or tingling in your hands?
10. *(app)* Are you pregnant or within 6 months post-partum?
11. *(app)* Do you know your blood pressure, and was the last reading ≥ 140/90?

### Rules for our app
- **All NO on 1–7 ⇒ cleared**, start immediately, no further gating. Show the PAR-Q+ "delay if…" caveats: temporary illness (cold/fever) → wait until better; pregnancy → talk to a health-care practitioner first; health changes → re-screen.
- **Any YES on 1, 2, 3 or 7 ⇒ hard gate.** Block the program; show "see a doctor before starting" plus a printable answer summary. No skip button.
- **YES on 4 or 5 ⇒ soft gate**: show the condition-specific PAR-Q+ follow-up path and recommend clearance before starting — the user is inactive, so the ACSM algorithm routes known cardiac/metabolic/renal disease to clearance before *any* exercise.
- **Q11 ≥ 140/90, or unknown ⇒ advise a BP check within 2 weeks**; cap intensity at sentence-talk (VT1) and RPE ≤ 6 until known. Do not block.
- **BMI alone is not a gate.** BMI 30–35 with all-NO answers is a green light; the evidence says lower the per-rep load, not the participation ([Janney & Jakicic 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2818622/)) [PR].
- **Q8 YES ⇒** force the scapular prerequisite block and lock the push progression at incline ≥ chest height for ≥ 4 weeks. **Q9 YES ⇒** default all hand-loaded work to fists / forearms / vertical surface. **Q10 YES ⇒** clinician clearance first.
- Re-run the screen every 12 months (PAR-Q+ clearance validity) or whenever the user reports a health change.

## A5. DOMS vs. injury pain

| Feature | DOMS (normal) | Warning (stop / modify) |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | 8–48 h after, peaks 24–72 h | during, or immediately after, the movement |
| Quality | dull, diffuse, tight/stiff ache | sharp, stabbing, burning, electric, numb/tingling |
| Location | belly of large muscles, usually **both** sides | one side only, or **in a joint** (knee, wrist, shoulder line) |
| With warm-up | eases once you move | unchanged or worse |
| Trajectory | better each day, gone in 3–7 days | same or worse after 72 h |
| Swelling / locking / giving way | no | yes ⇒ stop, see a clinician |

Sources: [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/delayed-onset-muscle-soreness) [G], [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Delayed_Onset_Muscle_Soreness) [E], [DOMS vs injury-related pain (ACE Health Professionals)](https://acehp.com.au/2022/09/19/delayed-onset-muscle-soreness-doms-vs-injury-related-pain/) [E].

### Rules for our app
- Two-tap triage on the pre- and post-session screens: *where* (muscle / joint) and *what* (ache / sharp). Muscle + ache ⇒ train, applying the readiness modifiers below. **Joint or sharp ⇒ regress that pattern one level today; if it recurs next session, block that pattern and suggest a clinician.**
- Never call DOMS "damage" or "proof it worked". Call it normal and expected in the first two weeks, and say it shrinks fast (repeated-bout effect).
- Any numbness or tingling in the hands during hand-loaded work ⇒ switch immediately to fists/forearm variants for the rest of the block and surface the Q9 follow-up.

# PART B — Adaptive / autoregulation rules (implementation spec)

**Design position taken from the evidence.** Subjective (RPE/RIR-based) and objective autoregulation produce comparable strength gains ([SR, IJERPH 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7810043/); [SR/MA, Sports Med 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8762534/)) [PR], and HRV-guided programming matched predetermined programming on outcomes **with less hard training** ([HIFT RCT 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8705715/); [SR/MA, Appl Sci 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/23/8532)) [PR]. Since novice RIR is unreliable, **our autoregulation inputs are form-break, completed reps, pain location, sleep, soreness and mood — not RIR.**

**Progression/regression base.** Double progression is the standard: fill the rep range, then advance the variation ([double progression explainer](https://legionathletics.com/double-progression/)) [E]. Community conventions: r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine — 3×5–8; hit 3×8 with good form, move up and restart at 3×5; stop the set when form breaks down ([RR wiki](https://redditbwf.github.io/wiki/recommended_routine.html)) [C]; Hybrid Calisthenics — "when you [can] hit the Level 3 standards for an exercise, move on to the next progression" ([hybridcalisthenics.com](https://www.hybridcalisthenics.com/pushups)) [C]. No RCT fixes the exact number of clean sessions required; **2 consecutive sessions** is the convention we adopt because it filters out a single good day without stalling motivation.

## Rule table

| Rule name | Trigger | Action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| PROGRESS_UP | 2 **consecutive** sessions hitting top of rep range on **all** working sets, no form-break flag, pain ≤ 1/5 | Advance that pattern one level; reset to bottom of rep range | Double progression [E]; RR 3×5→3×8 [C]; HC Level-3 standard [C]; [ACSM progression models](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/) [G] |
| PROGRESS_HOLD | Rep target met but a form-break flag was logged | Stay at level; repeat next session | Stop-on-form-break [C]; [novice RIR error ~4–5 reps](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/) [PR] |
| REGRESS_LEVEL | **2 consecutive** sessions with total reps < 70% of target, **or** form-break on ≥ 2 sets, **or** any joint/sharp pain in that pattern | Drop one level (or +1 incline notch); reset to bottom of range; copy says "level changed, not you" | [Autoregulation SR](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7810043/) [PR]; [no need to grind past ~2 RIR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/) [PR] |
| REGRESS_IMMEDIATE | Joint-line or sharp pain, or hand numbness/tingling, during a set | End that set; substitute the pain-free variant (fists / forearms / higher incline) for the rest of the session | [DOMS-vs-injury triage](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/delayed-onset-muscle-soreness) [G]/[E]; [wrist hyperextension loading](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0894113017301217) [PR] |
| SLEEP_LOW | Reported sleep < 6 h | −1 set per exercise; PROGRESS_UP disabled today; intensity cap RPE 6 | < 8 h sleep ≈ **1.7×** injury risk; > 8 h ⇒ 61% lower injury odds ([narrative review 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10745648/)) [PR]; [sleep-deprivation performance MA 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11996801/) [PR] |
| SLEEP_MID | Sleep 6–7 h, or self-rated sleep quality ≤ 2/5 | Keep sets; disable PROGRESS_UP today (a PR on poor sleep is a false positive) | as above [PR]; [HRV-guided ≈ predetermined with less load](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8705715/) [PR] |
| SORENESS_HIGH | Soreness ≥ 4/5 in a movement pattern | Regress **that pattern only**, one level, **today only**; auto-restore next session | [DOMS impairs force and alters recruitment](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Delayed_Onset_Muscle_Soreness) [PR]/[E]; targeted not global backoff [E] |
| SORENESS_PERSIST | Soreness ≥ 4/5 in the same pattern for 3 consecutive sessions | Treat as overreach: hold that pattern at its current level for 1 week and cut its volume 50% | Reactive deload evidence below [PR]/[E] |
| MOOD_LOW | Mood / motivation ≤ 2/5 | Offer the **minimum viable session**: 1 set per exercise, ~5 min — counts as a full session for streak purposes | [Minimal-dose RT overview: single-set RT and "snacks" raise strength](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11127831/) [PR]; [exercise-snack feasibility SR](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12732512/) [PR] |
| MIN_SESSION_ALWAYS | Any session start | The "2-minute / 1-set" option is permanently visible and never framed as failure | Micro-dose adherence/acceptability [PR]; [body shame → up to 42% dropout](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10215-8) [PR] |
| DELOAD_REACTIVE | Any of: 2 REGRESS_LEVEL events within 3 weeks; SORENESS_PERSIST; 3 consecutive sessions with a form-break flag | 1 week at ~50% sets, same levels, no progression attempts | Deloads at weeks 4 & 8 (1×/wk, 2 sets) did **not** impair hypertrophy or strength-endurance in untrained men ([Sci Rep 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40612-5)) [PR]; a scheduled 1-wk deload gave no benefit and slightly *less* lower-body strength ([2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10809978/)) [PR] |
| DELOAD_SCHEDULED | *Not implemented for weeks 1–12* | None — reactive only; revisit after week 12 | Deloads are non-harmful but non-superior in novices [PR]; coaches deload novices proactively mostly by convention ([Front Sports Act Living 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9811819/)) [E] |
| MISS_1_SESSION | 1 session missed | No change; resume at the same level; do not mention the miss | No measurable detraining at this scale [PR] |
| MISS_1_WEEK | 7–13 days with no session | Resume at same level, −1 set for the first session back; full volume next session | [1 session/wk (even 1 set) maintains strength for weeks–months](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11280775/) [PR] |
| MISS_2_3_WEEKS | 14–27 days | Drop 1 level in every pattern for **one** session, then re-test; restore the level if the rep target is met | [Detraining SR: strength largely retained over short layoffs](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-0413/1/1/1) [PR] |
| MISS_4_PLUS_WEEKS | ≥ 28 days | Drop 1–2 levels; rebuild via PROGRESS_UP (one level per 2 clean sessions ⇒ back in ~2–4 weeks); re-run the health screen | Retraining is far faster than initial training ([muscle-memory evidence](https://theconversation.com/muscle-memory-is-key-to-helping-you-get-in-shape-again-after-time-away-from-exercise-200583)) [PR]/[E]; [PAR-Q+ invalid after a health change](https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/January2020PARQPlusFillable.pdf) [G] |
| INTENSITY_CEILING | Weeks 1–8, always | Working sets capped at RPE 5–6 (wk 1–2), 6–7 (wk 3–5), 7 (wk 6–8); no failure sets | [RPE 6 validated novice start](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9099373/) [PR]; [benefit plateaus past ~2 RIR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/) [PR] |
| AEROBIC_CEILING | Any conditioning block | Pace so a full sentence is speakable (VT1 ≈ talk-difficulty VAS 2–3) | [Modified talk test validated in BMI ~31 inactive adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8017038/) [PR] |
| HARD_STOP | Chest pain/pressure, dizziness / near-faint, cold sweat, pallor, nausea with breathlessness, disproportionate dyspnoea | End session immediately; show the medical-care screen; block the next session until acknowledged | [AHA/ACSM exercise-termination indications](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hc3901.095960) [G] |
| SHAME_GUARD | Any REGRESS_* or missed-session event | Copy never uses "failed", "missed", "behind"; regression is shown as "found your level" and the streak is preserved | [Body shame → controlled motivation → dropout](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10215-8) [PR]; [autonomy-supportive framing sustains persistence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12938453/) [PR] |

**Precedence when rules conflict:** HARD_STOP > REGRESS_IMMEDIATE > MISS_* > DELOAD_REACTIVE > SORENESS_* > SLEEP_* > MOOD_LOW > PROGRESS_UP. At most one automatic level change per pattern per session.

**Known evidence gaps — do not over-claim in UI copy.** The "2 clean sessions" progression threshold and the "70% of rep target" regression threshold are coaching conventions [C], not trial-derived. Deload scheduling in true novices rests on two small studies, both null. The sleep–injury data come mainly from youth and adolescent athletes and are extrapolated here. No study has tested readiness-modifier rules in an obese home-training population — treat the whole of Part B as a falsifiable first version and log outcomes against it.
